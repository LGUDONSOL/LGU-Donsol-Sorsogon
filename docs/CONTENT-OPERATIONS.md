# Donsol Tourism content operations

The website becomes an effective tourism-marketing service only when its information is reviewed continuously. This operating schedule assigns recurring work without allowing unverified updates, business favoritism, or expired media permissions.

## Roles

| Role | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Tourism Administrator | Owns visitor information, campaigns, advisories, events, and final tourism-content approval |
| Content Reviewer | Checks grammar, dates, links, accessibility, neutrality, and consistency before release |
| Media Rights Coordinator | Maintains permissions, attribution, expiry dates, source URLs, and removal contacts |
| Data Protection Officer or privacy reviewer | Reviews forms, analytics, cookies, processors, and personal-information handling |
| Website Administrator | Publishes approved releases, maintains backups, checks errors, and restores service when necessary |

One person may hold more than one role, but time-sensitive tourism claims and privacy-impacting changes should receive a second-person review.

## Review schedule

### Weekly

- Check urgent advisories, weather-sensitive notices, activity interruptions, and event changes.
- Confirm that Tourism Office email, telephone, Messenger, directions, and feedback channels work.
- Review public feedback for corrections or accessibility barriers.

### Monthly

- Test all external image, video, map, business, and social links.
- Review attraction guides, itineraries, transport wording, directories, and event information.
- Confirm media permissions and remove anything expired or disputed.
- Review search and campaign performance after approved measurement is active.
- Record the date, reviewer, pages checked, corrections made, and unresolved items.

### Quarterly

- Confirm office hours, emergency guidance, business-directory status, and responsible-tourism rules.
- Review the Privacy Notice, Accessibility Statement, Disclaimer, Media Credits notice, and Site Map.
- Test keyboard navigation, 200% zoom, mobile layout, reduced motion, captions or text alternatives, and color contrast.
- Review whether Filipino or Bikol translations should be added or updated based on visitor needs.

### Before each campaign or peak period

- Verify all campaign claims, dates, wildlife language, registration guidance, links, and contact capacity.
- Prepare campaign URLs using the approved measurement naming standard.
- Confirm that every campaign photograph and video has current public-information permission.
- Prepare an update and removal plan if a featured activity changes or becomes unavailable.

### Whale shark seasonal checkpoints

- By October, reconfirm the coming season's operating dates, registration process, fees, safety rules, and visitor capacity with the responsible local office before announcing that activities are open.
- From November to early June, review current whale shark activity and any weather, sea, safety, or conservation advisory at least weekly; never turn recent sighting reports into a promise of future sightings.
- Near the beginning and end of the season, keep the website's confirmation warning prominent because activity and sightings can be less predictable.
- After the season, retain the off-season warning and promote year-round river, kayaking, food, culture, and community experiences instead of implying that whale shark interaction is available.
- Apply approved changes consistently to the homepage, whale shark guide, travel guide, sitemap dates, social posts, and campaign materials.

## Publishing workflow

1. Draft content using confirmed information and identify every time-sensitive statement.
2. Attach the source or responsible office for each schedule, rate, rule, event, listing, or advisory.
3. Review accessibility, privacy, media rights, neutrality, wildlife language, and mobile readability.
4. Obtain Tourism Administrator approval and any required subject-matter approval.
5. Back up the current release and publish only approved visitor-facing files.
6. Check the public URL, main actions, metadata, sitemap, and external media immediately after release.
7. Record the release date, approver, changed pages, and next review date.

## Content rules

- Never guarantee wildlife sightings, weather, availability, or uninterrupted operations.
- Never publish a rate, schedule, or event date without a source and review date.
- Keep business directories alphabetical or otherwise neutral; do not imply ranking or endorsement.
- Do not publish personal contact information without documented authority.
- Keep important instructions in text rather than only in an image, poster, or video.
- Require accurate alternative text, captions or equivalent summaries, and meaningful link labels.
- Use Filipino or Bikol translations only after review by a fluent, authorized human reviewer.

## Emergency update procedure

When visitor safety or a government advisory requires an urgent change:

1. Publish a short, dated notice from the responsible authority.
2. State what is affected, the effective time, the official contact, and when the notice will be reviewed.
3. Remove promotional calls to action that conflict with the advisory.
4. Update or remove the notice promptly when the authority changes its guidance.
5. Preserve the official record under the municipality's records-retention procedure.
